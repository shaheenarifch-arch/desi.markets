Essential Indian Spices: A Pantry Starter Guide

🌶️

You do not need dozens of jars to cook good Indian food. A small set of whole and ground spices covers most everyday dishes, and you can add more as you go.

Quick answer: Start with cumin seeds, mustard seeds, turmeric, red chilli powder, coriander powder and garam masala. Add cardamom, cloves, cinnamon and bay leaves for rice dishes and chai.

A starter spice list

  • Whole: cumin seeds, black mustard seeds, green cardamom, cloves, cinnamon, bay leaves.
  • Ground: turmeric, red chilli (Kashmiri is milder and redder), coriander, cumin.
  • Blends: garam masala for finishing; chaat masala for snacks and fruit.

Whole vs ground

  • Whole spices keep their flavour longer and are usually fried in oil at the start of cooking.
  • Ground spices cook quickly and can burn—add them after onions or with a splash of water.
  • Toast and grind small batches of whole spices for the freshest flavour.

Keeping spices fresh

  • Store in airtight jars away from heat, light and steam.
  • Buy smaller packs of spices you use rarely.
  • Ground spices lose aroma after several months; smell them—if there is little scent, replace them.

Frequently asked questions

What are the essential Indian spices for beginners?

Cumin, mustard seeds, turmeric, red chilli powder, coriander powder and garam masala cover most everyday recipes.

How long do ground spices last?

They are safe for a long time but lose aroma within months. Store them airtight and replace them when the smell fades.

What is the difference between garam masala and curry powder?

Garam masala is a warm finishing blend used in Indian cooking; curry powder is a Western-style all-purpose blend that usually includes turmeric.
